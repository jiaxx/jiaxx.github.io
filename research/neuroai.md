summary: >-
  We use artificial neural networks as explicit, testable hypotheses about biological vision, and we use their failures to identify principles that may improve both neuroscience and AI. Our [multi-scale model of mouse primary visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.040) integrates structural and physiological measurements, our work aligning [in vitro, in vivo, and in silico cell classes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37844-8) tests whether models preserve meaningful biological organization, and our benchmark of [unsupervised learning algorithms against human learning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=c0l2YolqD2T&noteId=WuvQmP-KBPD) evaluates models beyond static task accuracy. We now ask which objectives and architectures produce brain-like selectivity and invariance, whether predictions generalize across animals, stimuli, and states, and which biological principles improve continual learning, efficiency, and interpretability in AI systems.
